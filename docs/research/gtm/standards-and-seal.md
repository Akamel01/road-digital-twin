# Standards and seal rules for an in-service road safety review in BC

Issue #474 (part of map #485). Researched 2026-11-24. Sources were read, or where noted seen only in search results, on 2026-11-24; each claim links its source and gives that access date.

## Answer

- The national guidance for reviews on existing roads is the TAC *Canadian Guide to In-service Road Safety Reviews* (2004, 232 pp, CRaSH series) ([TAC in-service guide](https://www.tac-atc.ca/en/publications/ptm-inservice), accessed 2026-11-24). TAC describes both CRaSH guides as national guidelines.
- BC's Ministry of Transportation and Transit (MoT) road safety audit policy T-02/04 (March 8, 2004) is design-stage only — planning, preliminary design, detailed design and pre-opening — and does not cover construction or in-service work; projects under $100,000 are exempt ([BC MoT T-02/04](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/driving-and-transportation/transportation-infrastructure/engineering-standards-and-guidelines/technical-circulars/2004/t02-04a.pdf), accessed 2026-11-24). In the sources listed here, no newer BC MoT in-service review standard appears as of 2026-11-24.
- The *Manual of Uniform Traffic Control Devices for Canada* (MUTCDC) is the 6th edition, 2021, published 2021-06-17, 997 pp, TAC; it is not a regulatory standard that agencies must follow ([TAC MUTCDC product page](https://www.tac-atc.ca/en/knowledge-centre/technical-resources-search/publications/ptm-mutcdc21-e/), accessed 2026-11-24). BC and municipalities supplement it through the provincial Manual of Standard Traffic Signs and Pavement Markings ([BC MoSTS&PM](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/transportation/transportation-infrastructure/engineering-standards-guidelines/traffic-engineering-safety/traffic-signs-markings), accessed 2026-11-24) and traffic/parking bylaws (see §2.4).
- A P.Eng. seal (authentication) follows EGBC's three-question test and covers engineering findings and opinions; whether a purely factual data or inventory deliverable needs authentication is a judgment call for the engineer of record, because EGBC's documents do not exempt "reports of factual data" ([EGBC authentication guide v5.0](https://www.egbc.ca/getmedia/bb8be418-ae51-4ea5-ad0d-2c671bcb1df0/EGBC-QMG-Authentication-of-Documents.pdf), accessed 2026-11-24).
- An engineer-in-training (EIT) cannot authenticate a document and cannot represent themselves as a professional registrant; they work under Direct Supervision that may decrease but must not disappear ([EGBC authentication guide v5.0](https://www.egbc.ca/getmedia/bb8be418-ae51-4ea5-ad0d-2c671bcb1df0/EGBC-QMG-Authentication-of-Documents.pdf), accessed 2026-11-24; [EGBC bylaws](https://www.egbc.ca/getmedia/ff95a29b-64f6-49c8-8000-e98c8479248e/Engineers-and-Geoscientists-BC-Bylaws.pdf), accessed 2026-11-24).
- Any firm that does engineering work in BC, including sole practitioners and internal-use work, needs a Permit to Practice with a Responsible Registrant, a Responsible Officer, a permit number on all professional documents and a Professional Practice Management Plan (PPMP) within 12 months ([EGBC authentication infographic](https://www.egbc.ca/getmedia/f7b46ea5-b585-42c4-a20a-d1b6e787c6e1/Authentication-Infographic.pdf), accessed 2026-11-24; [EGBC firm practice overview](https://www.egbc.ca/Firms/Firm-Practice/Firm-Practice-Overview), accessed 2026-11-24).
- Safe System framing citable in a pitch: Canada's Road Safety Strategy 2035+ (RSS 2035+), approved 2026-02-20, with the vision "Towards Zero: A Safe Road System for All Canadians" and six Safe System principles; BC's Road Safety Strategy 2025 adopts Vision Zero and the Safe System approach ([BCRSS 2025](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/transportation/driving-and-cycling/roadsafetybc/strategy), accessed 2026-11-24; [CCMTA RSS 2035+](https://www.ccmta.ca/road-safety-strategy-2026/en/strategy), accessed 2026-11-24).

## 1. TAC guidance for in-service reviews and road safety audits

### 1.1 The two CRaSH guides

TAC publishes two guides under the Canadian Road Safety Engineering Handbook (CRaSH), a series of 10 titles developed under the Road Safety Standing Committee of the Chief Engineers' Council ([TAC in-service guide](https://www.tac-atc.ca/en/publications/ptm-inservice), accessed 2026-11-24). TAC describes them as national guidelines ([TAC audit guide](https://www.tac-atc.ca/en/knowledge-centre/technical-resources-search/publications/ptm-crsag-e), accessed 2026-11-24). The in-service guide covers existing roads; the audit guide covers design and new-project stages.

| Guide | Title | Edition/year | Pages | Member price (CAD) | Regular price (CAD) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| In-service review | Canadian Guide to In-service Road Safety Reviews | 2004 (single edition; no 2nd edition found on the TAC site as of 2026-11-24) | 232 | $169.00 | $239.00 | [TAC in-service guide](https://www.tac-atc.ca/en/publications/ptm-inservice) (accessed 2026-11-24) |
| Road safety audit | The Canadian Road Safety Audit Guide | 2001 (single edition; no newer edition found on the TAC site as of 2026-11-24) | 81 | $150.00 | $199.00 | [TAC audit guide](https://www.tac-atc.ca/en/knowledge-centre/technical-resources-search/publications/ptm-crsag-e) (accessed 2026-11-24) |

Quote (in-service guide page, https://www.tac-atc.ca/en/publications/ptm-inservice, accessed 2026-11-24): "The Canadian Guide to In-service Road Safety Reviews is a practical guide for conducting reviews on existing roads… A companion guide to TAC's Canadian Road Safety Audit Guide." Quote (audit guide page, https://www.tac-atc.ca/en/knowledge-centre/technical-resources-search/publications/ptm-crsag-e, accessed 2026-11-24): "This practical work is a comprehensive review of the safety audit process intended for road authorities, consultants and other interested groups." Publication codes PTM-INSERVICE-E and PTM-CRSAG-E; sold via the TAC online bookstore (print plus ebook).

The distinction: a road safety audit (RSA) runs at design and new-project stages — planning, preliminary design, detailed design and pre-opening — and checks a design against standards. An in-service review runs on existing roads and is crash-driven: it identifies factors leading to crashes that have occurred, or carry a high risk of occurring, and finds cost-effective countermeasures. Quote (Action2Zero, 2024-11-28, secondary, https://action2zero.tirf.ca/modules/road-safety-engineering/engineering-audits-and-in-service-reviews, accessed 2026-11-24): "An in-service road safety review is similar to an RSA but is conducted on an existing location within the road network. The purpose is not to confirm a specific section of the road infrastructure complies with design standards." 

### 1.2 What the in-service review report must contain

TAC publishes the in-service guide's chapter list openly, not a report template. The guide is divided into: Introduction; Safety Management Systems; Data Management; Network Screening; Engineering Analysis, Countermeasures; Evaluating Road Safety Improvements; and Getting Started ([TAC in-service guide](https://www.tac-atc.ca/en/publications/ptm-inservice), accessed 2026-11-24).

A typical report contents list comes from secondary evidence. Quote (Manitoba WSP in-service road safety review report, 2023-12-22, secondary, https://www.gov.mb.ca/mti/projects_management/pdf/pth1-pth5-in-service-road-safety-review-complete-report.pdf, accessed 2026-11-24): "The ISRSR was conducted in accordance with the Transportation Association of Canada (TAC) Canadian Guide to In-service Road Safety Reviews and was an independent and formal process, conducted by a multidisciplinary team who, based on their experience and expertise, provided opinions on safety issues from the perspective of all road users." This is a labelled example of practice, not a TAC requirement. TAC publishes the chapter list, not a report template.

### 1.3 Team roles

The TAC audit guide covers audit teams as a chapter ([TAC audit guide](https://www.tac-atc.ca/en/knowledge-centre/technical-resources-search/publications/ptm-crsag-e), accessed 2026-11-24). Quote (Manitoba WSP report, 2023-12-22, secondary, https://www.gov.mb.ca/mti/projects_management/pdf/pth1-pth5-in-service-road-safety-review-complete-report.pdf, accessed 2026-11-24): "an independent and formal process, conducted by a multidisciplinary team who, based on their experience and expertise, provided opinions on safety issues from the perspective of all road users." Quote (Action2Zero, 2024-11-28, secondary): "At a minimum, teams should be comprised of experience in road safety analysis, including crash data, understanding of traffic operations and data, and knowledge of human factors." In the sources listed here, no requirement appears that the team leader be a P.Eng.; the report is sealed by P.Eng.s in practice. The Manitoba report's five P.Eng. signatures are an example of practice, not a rule.

### 1.4 BC Ministry of Transportation and Transit guidance

BC MoT's Technical Circular T-02/04 (RSA policy, dated March 8, 2004) requires road safety audits to follow the TAC Canadian Road Safety Audit Guide, with MoT guidelines supplementing it. Quote: "Road Safety Audits are required to be carried out generally in accordance with the Transportation Association of Canada (TAC) publication, Canadian Road Safety Audit Guide. The MoT Guidelines supplement the road safety audit process and procedures described in the Canadian Road Safety Audit Guide." And: "Road transportation projects shall be subject to the Road Safety Audit (RSA) process as prescribed by Technical Circular T-02/04." ([BC MoT T-02/04](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/driving-and-transportation/transportation-infrastructure/engineering-standards-and-guidelines/technical-circulars/2004/t02-04a.pdf), accessed 2026-11-24; companion guidelines [T-02/04c](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/driving-and-transportation/transportation-infrastructure/engineering-standards-and-guidelines/technical-circulars/2004/t02-04c.pdf), accessed 2026-11-24.)

Scope and exclusions: RSA stages are planning, preliminary design, detailed design and pre-opening. Quote: "These Procedures do not cover road maintenance or temporary road projects… They likewise do not address resurfacing projects where the horizontal, vertical and cross sectional elements do not change." And: "At this time, the Ministry does not intend to conduct RSA at these stages" (construction and in-service). Threshold: "Projects with a value less than $100,000 are not required to have an RSA." Response report: the project team prepares a detailed response; both the RSA and the response report go to the RSA Committee.

Other BC documents that touch in-service work: the Draft Guidelines for Conducting a Corridor Management Plan Safety Review (BC MoT, developed 2001, revised July 2004, November 2010, August 2018), which applies to corridor- and project-level in-service safety review on provincial highways using collision-prone-location screening (collision rate above critical collision rate, or severity ratio above threshold and frequency above threshold), stakeholder input and countermeasure options; it is dated 2018 and marked "Draft" ([corridor safety review guidelines](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/driving-and-transportation/transportation-infrastructure/planning/guidelines/corridor-management-safety-review-guidelines.pdf), accessed 2026-11-24). The BC Community Road Safety Toolkit (2018-03-06) cites both TAC guides as resources (RSA Guide 2001 and the in-service guide 2004) ([community road safety toolkit](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/driving-and-transportation/driving/consequences/vision-zero/resource-kit-community-road-safety-toolkit-module3.pdf), accessed 2026-11-24).

In the sources listed here, no current BC in-service review policy was found. The BC documents found that address in-service work are the 2018 draft corridor guidelines and the Community Road Safety Toolkit's citation of the TAC 2004 guide. The formal BC RSA policy is design-stage only. In the sources listed here, no newer BC MoT in-service review standard appears as of 2026-11-24.

## 3. EGBC rules

### 3.1 Which deliverables need a P.Eng. seal

EGBC's governing rule is the three-question authentication test, from the Practice Advisor FAQs and the Quality Management Guide (QMG) v5.0. Quote (Practice Advisor FAQs, https://www.egbc.ca/Registrants/Practice-Resources/Practice-Advice-Program/Practice-Advisor-Faqs, accessed 2026-11-24): "If the response to all three questions above is yes, then the professional registrant must authenticate the document prior to the document being delivered." The three questions: (1) Does the document contain information resulting from the practice of professional engineering or professional geoscience? (2) Is the document complete for its intended purpose? (3) Will the document be relied upon by others (whether for bidding, permitting, construction, implementation, use or other reliance)? Quote (QMG v5.0, https://www.egbc.ca/getmedia/bb8be418-ae51-4ea5-ad0d-2c671bcb1df0/EGBC-QMG-Authentication-of-Documents.pdf, accessed 2026-11-24): "A Professional Registrant must Authenticate (a) a Document that: (i) the Professional Registrant has prepared in their professional capacity or has been prepared under their Direct Supervision, (ii) contains content related to the Regulated Practice, and (iii) will be relied on by others." And: "Only a Professional Registrant may Authenticate a Document."

Applied to the pilot deliverables: engineering findings, reports, opinions, review findings against TAC and MUTCDC, countermeasures and risk ranking must be authenticated by a P.Eng. who prepared them or under whose direct supervision they were prepared. A factual data or inventory deliverable — features found, typed and placed, with no engineering opinion — may not require authentication under the three-question test unless it will be relied upon by others for engineering decisions. Whether it needs a seal is a judgment call for the engineer of record. In the EGBC documents listed here, no "reports of factual data" exemption appears, and EGBC does not use an "engineering reports vs reports of factual data" dichotomy; the governing test is the three-question test above. EGBC does not approve sealing documents that do not contain engineering or geoscience content unless required by other federal or provincial legislation. Quote (QMG v5.0, §3.2.6.1, https://www.egbc.ca/getmedia/bb8be418-ae51-4ea5-ad0d-2c671bcb1df0/EGBC-QMG-Authentication-of-Documents.pdf, accessed 2026-11-24): "Professional Registrants must not Authenticate Documents that do not contain professional engineering or professional geoscience content, unless the Documents are required to be Authenticated by applicable legislation, associated regulations, the Bylaws, or policies endorsed by Engineers and Geoscientists BC Board." The 2018 sealing-insurance advisory is cited only as a dated example ([Practice Advisory: Sealing Insurance Declarations](https://www.egbc.ca/getmedia/9467401d-6d6b-4332-b469-367c2e028d6e/Practice-Advisory-Sealing-Insurance-Declarations.pdf.aspx), accessed 2026-11-24), subject to the HTTP 403 noted in Limits 5. The Manitoba in-service review report (secondary) was signed by five P.Eng.s, treating review findings as engineering opinion — an example of practice, not a rule.

### 3.2 What an EIT may do

Bylaw (EGBC Bylaws, in force 2026-05-15, https://www.egbc.ca/getmedia/ff95a29b-64f6-49c8-8000-e98c8479248e/Engineers-and-Geoscientists-BC-Bylaws.pdf, accessed 2026-11-24): "An engineer-in-training must not represent themselves as a Professional Registrant." And: "An engineer-in-training must not engage in any area of the Reserved Practice except under the Direct Supervision of an individual who is legally entitled to independently practise in that area of the Reserved Practice, and that area of the Reserved Practice is of the type that may be provided under supervision pursuant to section 54(2) of the PGA." 
Direct Supervision guide (EGBC v4.0, published 2026-09-01): "It is expected that Subordinates who are EITs or GITs working their way through the competency building and training process will be granted increasing autonomy, independence, and responsibility over time. While the level of Direct Supervision an EIT or GIT receives may decrease during the training process, it must not disappear entirely." And: "even with very experienced and capable Subordinates, the Professional Registrant who is Directly Supervising the work must remain involved and continue oversight of the professional engineering or professional geoscience work or decisions." ([EGBC direct supervision guide](https://www.egbc.ca/getmedia/4508c002-9c35-4e4a-9750-0141f11aca49/EGBC-QMG-Direct-Supervision.pdf), accessed 2026-11-24.)

### 3.3 Firm permit to practise

When a firm needs one ([EGBC firm practice overview](https://www.egbc.ca/Firms/Firm-Practice/Firm-Practice-Overview), accessed 2026-11-24): "All firms that engage in the practice of professional engineering or geoscience as part of their operations require a Permit to Practice. This includes firms in the private and public sectors, as well as sole practitioners." And: "A Permit to Practice is required for any and all engineering or geoscience work in BC regardless of whether the work is minimal." It applies even if the work is internal-use only.

Structure: at least one Responsible Registrant (an engineering or geoscience professional who acknowledges responsibility for the firm, is the point of contact for practice reviews, audits and investigations, and must take the Regulation of Firms Permit to Practice Training) and one Responsible Officer (an executive lead who need not be a registrant, with authority to make binding decisions).

Obligations: Quote (EGBC authentication infographic, https://www.egbc.ca/getmedia/f7b46ea5-b585-42c4-a20a-d1b6e787c6e1/Authentication-Infographic.pdf, accessed 2026-11-24): "All authenticated documents, manual or digital, must have a visible Permit to Practice number for the Firm under which the work is being performed." Plus a Professional Practice Management Plan (PPMP) within 12 months, covering code of conduct, quality management policies and procedures, continuing education compliance, organizational structure, names of the Responsible Officer and Registrant(s), areas of practice, the designated Responsible Registrant per area, and the policy on use of the Permit to Practice number.

Fees (CAD, official, EGBC firm fees page, accessed 2026-11-24): multi-professional firm annual fee = √N × $500, where N is the number of EGBC registrants on staff; sole practitioner = $250 per year (50% discount); application fee $350 (non-refundable); late fee 15% of the annual fee for failure to pay. Quote: "A multi-professional firm's annual Permit to Practice registration fee is calculated using the following formula: √N x $500, where N is the number of Engineers and Geoscientists BC registrants on staff." And: "Sole practitioners receive a 50% discount resulting in a flat rate of $250 annually for the Permit to Practice registration fee." And: "An application fee of $350 is also required." Renewal: April 1 to May 31 annually. ([EGBC firm fees](https://www.egbc.ca/How-To-Apply/Register-As-A-Firm/Firm-Fees), accessed 2026-11-24; [firm practice overview](https://www.egbc.ca/Firms/Firm-Practice/Firm-Practice-Overview), accessed 2026-11-24.)

## 4. Safe System framing

### 4.1 CCMTA national strategy

Canada's Road Safety Strategy 2035+ (RSS 2035+), the fifth national framework, was approved by the Council of Ministers Responsible for Transportation and Highway Safety on 2026-02-20. Steward: the Canadian Council of Motor Transport Administrators (CCMTA), Ottawa. Vision: "Towards Zero: A Safe Road System for All Canadians." Quote (RSS 2035+ key messages, https://www.ccmta.ca/web/road-safety-strategy-2026/files/public/2026/RSS%202035+%20Key%20Messages.pdf, accessed 2026-11-24): "The Safe System Approach is an internationally recognized framework that acknowledges the inevitability of human error and the need to design a transportation system that prevents those errors from resulting in death or serious injury." Safe System Approach (SSA) principles (quote, RSS 2035+ info sheet): "Deaths and serious injuries are unacceptable; People Make Mistakes; Human Vulnerability; Shared Responsibility; Redundancy and Layered Protection; Proactive and Equitable Design." ([CCMTA RSS 2035+](https://www.ccmta.ca/road-safety-strategy-2026/en/strategy), accessed 2026-11-24; [info sheet](https://www.ccmta.ca/web/road-safety-strategy-2026/files/public/2026/RSS%202035+%20Info%20Sheet.pdf), accessed 2026-11-24.)

## Limits

1. The TAC in-service (2004) and RSA (2001) guides are paywalled ([in-service](https://www.tac-atc.ca/en/publications/ptm-inservice), [audit](https://www.tac-atc.ca/en/knowledge-centre/technical-resources-search/publications/ptm-crsag-e), accessed 2026-11-24). Only their public section and chapter lists and metadata were verified; finer template, independence and team-composition requirements inside the guides are not verified. In the sources listed here, no 2nd edition appears as of 2026-11-24.
2. The MUTCDC 6th edition full text is paywalled; it is sold in print and e-book only ([TAC MUTCDC product page](https://www.tac-atc.ca/en/knowledge-centre/technical-resources-search/publications/ptm-mutcdc21-e/), accessed 2026-11-24). The errata verifies the clause numbers and titles used here, including C3.1, C3.1.1, C7.2.1–C7.2.3 and C3.5; the full text (dimensions and warrants) is paywalled. No MUTCDC clause beyond the clauses listed above is asserted.
3. In the sources listed here, no city-specific marking or sign engineering manual appears for Coquitlam, Surrey or Richmond. Vancouver's [Engineering Design Manual](https://vancouver.ca/files/cov/engineering-design-manual.pdf) returned HTTP 403 on 2026-11-24 and again on a later check; the table wording is from the 2026-11-24 read and the markings statement is quoted from the search index. Municipal differences in this note are limited to bylaw curb/parking rules plus the two disclosed observations: Surrey's no-painted-curbs note and Vancouver's EDM line.
4. BC's [Manual of Standard Traffic Signs and Pavement Markings](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/transportation/transportation-infrastructure/engineering-standards-guidelines/traffic-engineering-safety/traffic-signs-markings) (accessed 2026-11-24) is a living web catalogue with no single consolidated edition number or list price; the page was reachable on 2026-11-24.
