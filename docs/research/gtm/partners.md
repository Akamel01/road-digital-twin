# Pilot partner research: BC university groups and engineering consultancies

## Purpose and method

This note answers issue #476 and is planning only: no organisation was contacted. It compares BC university groups and BC engineering consultancies as first-pilot partners for a small firm that delivers road-safety analysis, and it sets out how such partnerships are usually structured. Method: primary and public sources, with a claim-level inline citation after every factual sentence; criteria are stated and applied before ranking. Firm size figures are self-published (pay-transparency reports or marketing pages), not audited. Sources are cited inline and no separate source register was updated. A `published program figure` is a figure published on a program's own page, not a quote, list price or forum report. The first targets are Coquitlam, Surrey, Vancouver and Richmond, with a TAC in-service review plus MUTCDC context and a partner P.Eng. sealing deliverables (https://github.com/Akamel01/road-digital-twin/issues/485, accessed 2026-11-25).

## How "best" is defined

Six criteria apply. C1: road-safety research, conflict work or asset inventory. C2: sealing route (an individual P.Eng. seals BC deliverables) (https://github.com/Akamel01/road-digital-twin/issues/485, accessed 2026-11-25). C3: recent BC municipal work (published 2021-11-24 or later, or an undated page described as current). C4: accessibility to a small firm. C5: geographic fit (presence in Coquitlam, Surrey, Vancouver or Richmond is met; adjacent presence is partly met) (https://github.com/Akamel01/road-digital-twin/issues/485, accessed 2026-11-25). C6: verifiability (two distinct supporting organisations for the block's C1/C3/C4 claims; one organisation is partly met — one publisher). `adjacent` means shares a border with Coquitlam, Surrey, Vancouver or Richmond. `municipal-scale` means city-wide or a whole municipal asset class, such as every sidewalk or every signal in a city. `partly met` cases: C3 — project before 2021-11-24, or an undated page not clearly current; C4 — contact page but no reachable role title; C5 — adjacent presence only. Pass rule: a fabricated claim or a factual sentence with no citation fails the note; weak evidence never disqualifies an option. C1, C3, C4 and C5 may be partly met or no source found with a one-line reason. Exactly two university and three consultancy options are listed regardless of evidence strength, with limits disclosed.
Applicability matrix:

| Criterion | University option | Consultancy option | Program (Mitacs/NSERC) |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | road-safety research (including injury/conflict analysis) or municipal-scale asset-inventory research | road-safety review/conflict work or municipal-scale asset inventory | not applicable |
| C2 | proposed sealing route via a shortlisted consultancy and its cited P.Eng. role title; proposed, not sourced unless sourced; partly met if no such title exists | cited P.Eng. role title, or partly met with reason | not applicable |
| C3 | published 2021-11-24 or later, or undated page, described as current | same; provincial reviews reported but not counted | not applicable |
| C4 | published program terms/eligibility for the collaboration route; rates where published | published contact route + reachable role title; rates not required | program terms/eligibility; rates where published |
| C5 | institutional/project presence in the four cities (met) or adjacent (partly met) | office/project presence in the four cities (met) or adjacent (partly met) | not applicable |
| C6 | two distinct supporting organisations preferred; one organisation = partly met — one publisher | same | same |

Ranking: consultancies are ranked first, then universities. Compare C6 first (met before partly met — one publisher before no source found), then C2 (met before partly met), then C4, then C1, then C3 recency, then C5 proximity, then alphabetical by option name. University C2 is scored against the highest-ranked shortlisted consultancy that has a cited P.Eng. role title. Per the #485 premise, a partner P.Eng. seals BC deliverables (https://github.com/Akamel01/road-digital-twin/issues/485, accessed 2026-11-25).

## University groups in BC

### UBC Transportation Group (Department of Civil Engineering)

The formal unit is `The Transportation Group`, in the Department of Civil Engineering (https://civil.ubc.ca/research/research-areas/transportation-engineering/, accessed 2026-11-25). Focus areas include road safety, intelligent and automated transportation systems, travel behaviour and transportation planning, multimodal long-distance transportation systems, transportation asset management and traffic-related emissions and air quality (https://civil.ubc.ca/research/research-areas/transportation-engineering/, accessed 2026-11-25). The group has active research partnerships with the BC Ministry of Transportation and Infrastructure, ICBC, TransLink, municipalities throughout Western Canada, Transport Canada and other public and private entities (https://civil.ubc.ca/research/research-areas/transportation-engineering/, accessed 2026-11-25). A published municipal project is the automated traffic conflict analysis of a pedestrian crossing treatment at an intersection in Surrey, published in 2015 (https://www.academia.edu/127923800/Assessing_Safety_Improvements_to_Pedestrian_Crossings_Using_Automated_Conflict_Analysis, accessed 2026-11-25; the URL returns 403 to the reviewer, so the intersection detail rests on the paper metadata only). A second published municipal project is the before-and-after safety evaluation of a leading pedestrian interval in Surrey, published in 2015 (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/298972511_BEFORE-AFTER_AUTOMATED_SAFETY_ANALYSIS_OF_LEADING_PEDESTRIAN_INTERVAL_LPI, accessed 2026-11-25; the URL returns 403 to the reviewer, so the intersection count is not verified). Both are dated before 2021-11-24, so they do not satisfy C3. no recent public municipal project found in this pass.

## How these partnerships are usually structured

### Subcontract to the consultancy

The small firm is engaged by a consultancy as a specialist subcontractor; the consultancy may hold the municipal contract. not supported by a source found in this pass for prime/subcontract commercial terms (who holds the municipal contract, how the specialist is engaged, insurance/flow-down, payment).

### Consultancy as prime

The consultancy holds the prime municipal contract and engages the small firm as a subcontractor; the small firm's scope, insurance/flow-down and payment flow through the consultancy. not supported by a source found in this pass for prime/subcontract commercial terms (who holds the municipal contract, how the specialist is engaged, insurance/flow-down, payment).

### University collaboration through Mitacs

A research internship co-funded by a partner organisation and Mitacs. A standard Accelerate award is CAD 15,000 per four- or six-month internship, with a partner contribution starting at CAD 7,500 and Mitacs matching CAD 7,500, published program figure (https://www.mitacs.ca/our-programs/accelerate/, accessed 2026-11-25). Partner organisations can be for-profit corporations, not-for-profit corporations, hospitals, municipalities or Indigenous governments in Canada; projects with a not-for-profit, municipality or hospital partner must demonstrate an economic or productivity orientation (https://www.mitacs.ca/our-programs/accelerate/, accessed 2026-11-25). Mitacs takes no position on intellectual property; IP is shared between the academic institution, its researchers and the partner organisation according to the academic institution's IP rules, unless a separate agreement is negotiated (https://www.mitacs.ca/our-programs/accelerate/, accessed 2026-11-25). The agreement route: the partner pays its contribution on receipt of the Mitacs invoice, and Mitacs forwards the award to the academic institution only after payment; no work begins until an award letter is issued and funds are distributed (https://www.mitacs.ca/our-programs/accelerate/, accessed 2026-11-25).

### University collaboration through NSERC Alliance

A university research grant with partner cash or in-kind support. NSERC claims no ownership of intellectual property and recommends that the university and partner sign a research agreement defining IP rights and obligations (https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/funding-opportunity/alliance-advantage, accessed 2026-11-25). The agreement route: the university researcher applies, with at least one partner organisation recognised for cost sharing; NSERC contributes 66.7% of the project's direct costs (2:1 ratio) against recognised partner cash contributions (https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/funding-opportunity/alliance-advantage, accessed 2026-11-25). A private-sector partner must be registered or incorporated, have at least two full-time employees, carry out R&D or produce goods or services in Canada, operate from its own offices, and have the capacity to exploit the results (https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/funding-opportunity/alliance-advantage, accessed 2026-11-25). Companies with fewer than five full-time employees, or operating from a home or virtual address, must complete a supplemental information questionnaire (https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/funding-opportunity/alliance-advantage, accessed 2026-11-25).
