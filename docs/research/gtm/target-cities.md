# Target cities for a first pilot: Coquitlam, Surrey, Vancouver, Richmond

Scope: a research note comparing four Metro Vancouver cities as a first pilot for a measured 3D road digital twin of 3–5 intersections (map #470, 2026-11-24). "In-service road safety review" means a review of a road already in service; the repo calls the same activity a road safety audit. The term is used plainly and is not attributed to any standards body. All sources accessed on 2026-11-24; prices in CAD. Research only; no contact made. No vendor list price, quote or forum report was found; all figures are public budgets, grants or contract awards.

## 1. Coquitlam

## 5. Cost-sharing mechanics

A vendor cannot be the applicant for ICBC RIP or federal ATF; the applicant is the municipality/road authority (or Indigenous government), and the vendor is a named contractor procured after award under municipal procurement rules [66]. ICBC RIP is a cost-share: ICBC contribution plus municipality, ranked by crash history/high-risk locations; ICBC engineers do road-safety audits at no cost to municipalities that successfully apply (Municipal Road Safety Audit Program, part of RIP) [66][67]. Municipality or MoTI submits the application via GEN520 [66][68]. ICBC fixed public deadline: not found on icbc.com; the cycle is annual and Richmond reports submission before "its annual program deadline" with outcome in the following year's update [66]. CCBF/BCSF Community Stream: capital category "Local roads and bridges – roads, bridges and active transportation infrastructure" is eligible; studies are eligible only as asset-management studies (asset inventory, risk matrix) under Capacity Building, not collision studies or safety reviews [69][70]. In the Metro Vancouver Regional District, 95% of the regional fund goes to TransLink; cities still receive a CWF allocation [69][70]. BC Active Transportation Infrastructure Grants: paused for 2025/26 intake pending CleanBC review outcome; cost-share up to $500,000 CAD/project [71]. BC Vision Zero in Road Safety Grant Program: up to $20,000 CAD/project, no community contribution required, local governments/NGOs/Indigenous eligible, for-profit not eligible; 2026/2027 intake closed (deadline 2025-11-28), next intake expected fall 2026 [72]. Federal Active Transportation Fund: application period closed; future intakes may be announced; for-profit individuals/entities ineligible [73]. Canada Public Transit Fund (from 2026-27): $3B CAD/yr permanent; Targeted Funding stream includes active transportation calls with flexible intakes (details not yet published) [73].
